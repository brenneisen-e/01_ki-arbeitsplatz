The banking hall of a 1970s German savings bank, carefully modernised: a double-height room clad in shell limestone with a coffered board-formed concrete ceiling, terrazzo floor with thin brass divider strips, full-height oak-panelled doors with brass lever handles, and a tall steel-framed window wall with dark bronze mullions along the left side. A single open stair with a slim brass handrail rises at the back. It is 8:20 in the morning; low autumn sun enters through the window wall and lays four sharp bright rectangles across the terrazzo. Four people are present and small in the frame: one member of staff walking away from camera toward the stair, two customers seated far right on a plain oak bench, one person standing at a low stone counter. Everything looks orderly, used and unremarkable. Large-format architectural photography, 4x5 view camera with a 90mm lens, verticals perfectly corrected, deep focus at f/11, ISO 100 daylight colour film, tripod at 1.55 metre eye level, strictly frontal single-vanishing-point perspective. Daylight only, interior fixtures switched off. Muted near-neutral palette of limestone beige, concrete grey, oak brown, patinated brass, charcoal steel, cool grey-blue shadows. Calm balanced framing, generous empty space, even exposure corner to corner, no vignette, fine film grain. No text, no logos, no signage, no glowing screens, no teal, no cyan.
