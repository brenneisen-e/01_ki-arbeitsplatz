The main banking hall of a West German savings bank built in 1974, photographed at 8:40 in the morning. A two-storey room with a board-marked coffered concrete ceiling, oak-slat panelling along the rear wall, pale terrazzo floor with a thin brass inlay strip, and a grid of tall bronze-framed windows on the left. The low sun lays long warm rectangles across the floor. A patinated brass handrail runs up a limestone stair at the back. At a low oak counter a woman in a grey wool cardigan sets out a stack of paper forms; two customers in overcoats sit on an oxblood wool bench, one folding a newspaper. Nobody looks at the camera. Camera at standing eye level, off-centre, the right third of the frame empty floor. Shot on a Hasselblad medium-format camera on Kodak Portra 400 colour negative film, 50mm lens at f/5.6, 1/60s, handheld. Fine visible grain, natural restrained colour, slightly desaturated; single-direction daylight, cool blue-grey shadows, no artificial fill, no flare. Matte surfaces, dust in the air, honest skin texture. Unposed documentary colour photograph, no digital polish, no HDR. No text, no logos, no signage, no glowing screens, no teal, no cyan.
