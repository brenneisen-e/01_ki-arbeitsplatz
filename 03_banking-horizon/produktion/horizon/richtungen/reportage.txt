The customer hall of a 1970s German savings bank, refurbished, at 8:15 in the morning. Wide low-ceilinged room with a square grid ceiling of recessed fluorescent panels, washed-concrete columns on a six-metre grid, oak veneer wall panelling, terrazzo floor with brass expansion strips, narrow vertical windows in dark brown aluminium frames along the left wall. An older female employee in a grey cardigan unlocks a counter drawer at the right edge of the frame, half turned away, partly cut off by the frame. In the middle distance a man in his fifties waits standing with a folder under his arm, out of focus. Foreground: the back of a low partition and a paper cup on it. Cold morning daylight from the left mixes with warm fluorescent light overhead; the windows are blown out white. Nobody looks at the camera. Shot on a Leica M rangefinder, 35mm lens at f/2.8, Kodak Portra 400, handheld at standing eye level, available light only, no film lighting. Off-centre framing, horizon slightly tilted. Muted palette of grey concrete, oak, beige linoleum, off-white, one rust-orange folder spine. Flat negative-film contrast, visible grain, corner falloff, one focal plane. Documentary reportage photography. No text, no logos, no signage, no screens as light sources.
